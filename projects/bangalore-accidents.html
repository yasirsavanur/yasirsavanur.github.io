Safety events make more sense in place

A table of collision warnings can show frequency, but location gives the events operational context. Repeated warnings around the same roads or junctions may point towards a pattern worth investigating.

This project uses BMTC bus collision-avoidance data to explore the geographic distribution of events, identify hotspots and consider how effectively warnings support safer operation.

The dashboard is an investigation tool. A hotspot tells an analyst where to look more closely; it does not establish the cause of every warning at that location.

A signal for prioritisation

Used responsibly, the dashboard can help a transport analyst prioritise locations for deeper review and ask better questions about routes, junctions, operating conditions or warning-system behaviour.

The analysis is most useful when combined with exposure measures such as journeys or kilometres travelled, plus road geometry, time, weather and verified incident outcomes.

A warning is not automatically an accident

Collision-avoidance events show that the system detected a risky condition; they do not by themselves prove a crash occurred or that one party caused it. More warnings may also reflect more bus activity rather than a more dangerous road.

The dashboard should therefore guide investigation and safety planning, not be used as a standalone causal model.
